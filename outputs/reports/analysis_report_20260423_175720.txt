  For each face (detected / undetected / false positive), we measure:
    - Face area: bounding box width × height (pixels²)
        Example: 256 px² ≈ 16×16 pixel face (very tiny)
                 1,024 px² ≈ 32×32 pixel face (small)
                 4,096 px² ≈ 64×64 pixel face (medium)
    - Face brightness: mean value of HSV V-channel (0=black, 255=white)
        < 80 → dark face (hard to detect)
        80–180 → normal illumination
        > 180 → bright / overexposed
    - Image blur: variance of Laplacian operator on grayscale image
        < 50 → blurry image
        50–200 → moderate sharpness
        > 200 → sharp image
    - Confidence: model's certainty for its detection (0.0–1.0)

======================================================================
  5. KEY FINDINGS & RECOMMENDATIONS
======================================================================

  Based on the analysis above:

  [MTCNN]
    - 96% of missed faces are smaller than 32×32 pixels
    - Missed faces: mean area = 433 px²
    - Detected faces: mean area = 2,104 px²
    - The primary failure mode is FACE SIZE, not brightness or blur.

  [RetinaFace_insightface]
    - 98% of missed faces are smaller than 32×32 pixels
    - Missed faces: mean area = 301 px²
    - Detected faces: mean area = 1,829 px²
    - The primary failure mode is FACE SIZE, not brightness or blur.

  [RetinaFace_insightface_det_MultiScale]
    - 97% of missed faces are smaller than 32×32 pixels
    - Missed faces: mean area = 293 px²
    - Detected faces: mean area = 2,458 px²
    - The primary failure mode is FACE SIZE, not brightness or blur.
    - 21% of missed faces are in dark regions (V < 80)
      → CLAHE preprocessing may help for these cases.

  [RetinaFace_insightface_det_Tiled_MultiScale]
    - 96% of missed faces are smaller than 32×32 pixels
    - Missed faces: mean area = 389 px²
    - Detected faces: mean area = 1,732 px²
    - The primary failure mode is FACE SIZE, not brightness or blur.
    - 25% of missed faces are in dark regions (V < 80)
      → CLAHE preprocessing may help for these cases.

  General recommendations:
    1. Multi-scale inference with upscaling recovers ~10-25% more faces.
    2. Tiled inference helps on high-resolution crowd images.
    3. Sub-16×16 faces are below detector resolution limits;
       super-resolution preprocessing is needed for those.
    4. Lowering confidence threshold below 0.4 risks more false positives.
    5. Ensemble (RetinaFace + MTCNN) captures faces missed by either alone.
